# Large-Scale FFSP/JSSP Findings

## Research Question

Can original USW and ASW, from an identical strong checkpoint and fresh optimizer, outperform matched PO and BOPO at FFSP1000 and JSSP50x20 without materially degrading absolute solution quality?

## Current Understanding

Architectural scale transfer is not the main risk. MatNet parameters depend on the fixed three-stage/four-machine structure rather than the number of jobs, and MGL parameters depend on feature dimensions rather than the number of jobs or machines. The main risks are target-scale rollout/backward memory, throughput, objective saturation, and preserving a fair common initialization.

The clean comparison is checkpoint-backed continuation: select one strong common checkpoint per problem using only a fresh target validation set, clone it exactly, reset Adam, and expose every method to the same ordered target instances. This follows the successful routing pattern while removing initialization and optimizer-state confounds.

## Patterns and Insights

- Large scheduling scale should be measured in jobs/operations, not copied mechanically from routing node counts.
- Zero-shot compatibility does not establish trainability at B=128 or 24 starts; backward probes are mandatory.
- A fixed source-scale epoch count is misleading because target rollouts have very different costs. Optimizer-update gates plus validation promotion provide a more defensible continuation budget.
- JSSP50x20 is comfortably trainable in FP32 at the locked B=128 semantics, while FFSP1000's full 24-start graph sits beyond the 24 GiB FP32 boundary even at physical batch one. Precision, not candidate-count reduction, is the first allowed FFSP adaptation because the 24 machine-order starts are part of the protocol.
- BF16 full graph with cuDNN SDPA disabled is about 1.9x faster than exact FP32 replay at FFSP1000, while replay is the low-memory numerical-audit path. Both preserve the complete 24-candidate pool.
- More JSSP training is not uniformly beneficial: BOPO is stable, USW overfits after a transient step-300 recovery, and ASW is flat-to-worse. The duplicated PO artifact cannot support a formal learning-curve claim, although both observed sequences degrade sharply. Checkpoint selection must remain method-specific and validation-only.
- H3 calibration changes the best reachable validation point but not the instability pattern: low-LR USW is best at step300, alpha-zero ASW is best at step200, and both worsen by step500. Any paper-facing H3 comparison must use the locked intermediate checkpoints and describe the non-monotone trajectories.
- Across all dense Phase-B variants, lowering LR or removing weight decay did not remove the non-monotone deficit. Dense USW/ASW use about 8,100 instance-local pairs per update versus BOPO's 15 rank-stratified anchor pairs; pair geometry and gradient density are now the bounded mechanistic variable, while checkpoint, stream, B/K, physical batch, loss formula, and family-leading calibration remain fixed.
- H5 sparse USW narrows the best dense-USW gap from 5.093697 to 3.093720 and ends on its best gate, unlike the dense trajectories. Sparse pair geometry is therefore directionally useful but insufficient at the transferred LR; the next bounded variable is update scale, not another dense LR/WD/alpha retry.
- H6 shows that matching LR-times-gradient scale without changing USW's total-trajectory loss curvature is insufficient. The persistent H5/H6 mean gradient near 5.3 versus BOPO 0.569 mirrors the exact 10x increase from 100 source decisions to 1000 target decisions. H7 removes this scale excess as intended (mean gradient 0.517274) but still misses BOPO by 3.062500, ruling out trajectory scale as the sole cause.
- H8 shows that mean-one relative-gap temperature plus source-length normalization can beat BOPO's matched validation mean by 4.687523 at the equal 500-update budget. Its 15/32 win count and wide paired CI show that average improvement is carried by a minority of larger wins; cross-instance weighting consistency is now the bounded mechanism, not more H8 updates or another LR/WD sweep.
- H10 shows that ASW gap-regret weights cannot replace H8's relative-gap temperature: the best H10 mean misses BOPO by 3.375076. The next bounded mechanism should keep H8's gap emphasis but compress its linear temperature curvature to reduce extreme-pair pressure across heterogeneous instances.
- The dynamic eight-instance validation mean is an optimization proxy, not a paper-comparable number. Paper-facing JSSP claims must use official TA50x20 gaps with the same B' and greedy-injection protocol.

## Lessons and Constraints

- FFSP's current paper evaluation loader restores `num_job=100`; it must rebuild the environment for FFSP1000.
- JSSP checkpoint `allowed_shapes=[[15,15]]` does not block direct benchmark inference but must be overridden for target-scale dataloaders/continuation.
- Final tests cannot be used for checkpoint or hyperparameter selection.
- Any PO-anchored or otherwise mixed objective must be labeled a variant and cannot substitute for the original USW/ASW claim.
- A submission timeout is not evidence that a remote launch failed. Recheck the process table and output root before retrying; one run per root is mandatory even for one-update smokes.
- Phase-B dense-pair configurations are exhausted and must not be retried. Any next candidate must be explicitly labelled as an anchor-geometry USW/ASW variant rather than the original dense objective.

## Open Questions

- Do the locked validation-selected H3 checkpoints (USW step300 and ASW step200) improve official TA50x20 without violating the final-test discipline or absolute-quality guard?
- Does the BOPO paper gap close further under its published from-scratch budget (B=256, 20 epochs), rather than the deliberately matched 500-update continuation used here?
- Can a mean-one concave relative-gap temperature retain H8's matched-mean improvement while reducing its cross-instance heterogeneity?
- Can per-instance gradient clipping preserve H8's matched-mean advantage while reducing update outliers and paired heterogeneity?
